  - type: thesis
    mark: →
    title: Sell the audit, and let the audit sell the program.
    text: A prospect can't judge a search program until they've seen their own site diagnosed. A fixed-scope audit is an easy decision and useful even if the client stops there, and its top ten findings turn the program proposal into a conversation about their numbers instead of our credentials.
  - type: flow
    heading: The path
    intro: Six steps from first conversation to a signed program. Skipping ahead, usually by proposing a program before the audit, is how agencies end up selling on price.
    items:
      - title: Prepare for the first call
        text: Search a few of the prospect's category terms, run five category prompts in two AI assistants, and check that their priority pages appear in a site search. Bring one specific observation about their site; it earns more attention than a credentials deck.
      - title: Run discovery
        text: Work through the discovery questions below, and listen more than you pitch. The answers become the audit's kickoff notes.
      - title: Qualify
        text: Check the criteria below. When one is missing, change what you offer, not just the price.
      - title: Sell the audit
        text: "Send a one-page scope: the fixed fee, the two-week timeline, and the access needed in the first week. Once it's signed, hand the discovery notes to the account team, so the [kickoff](page:onboarding) confirms answers instead of asking them again."
      - title: Present the findings as the pitch
        text: A live working session with the budget holder in the room. The top ten findings, the opportunity model's range and the plays that fix them lead straight into the proposal, sent after the session.
      - title: Ask for the decision
        text: Agree a decision date in the session. A signed program starts with [Baseline](phase:baseline). With no decision by the date, follow up with the finding that costs the client most while it waits.
  - type: table
    heading: Discovery questions
    intro: A 45-minute call. Ask in the prospect's language, and write down the answers word for word where you can; they reappear in the audit and the proposal.
    columns:
      - Ask
      - Why we ask
      - Listen for
    rows:
      - - What prompted you to look at this now?
        - The trigger decides which offer to lead with
        - A traffic drop, a redesign, a new marketing leader, a competitor, or an AI answer someone didn't like
      - - Who are your best customers, and what do they buy?
        - Sets the priority products, topics and buyer stages
        - Clear segments and products, not "everyone"
      - - What's a typical deal worth, and how long does it take to close?
        - Lead value for the opportunity model, and how long results take to reach pipeline
        - Numbers they know, or the name of someone in sales who does
      - - Who do you lose deals to?
        - Named competitors for the benchmark
        - Sales competitors. We'll add the search competitors ourselves.
      - - What have you tried in search before, and what happened?
        - Past agencies and results, and what they'll be skeptical of
        - What the reports showed, and whether they led to any decisions
      - - How would you know today that a lead came from search?
        - Whether pipeline can be reported
        - Original source and landing page recorded in the CRM, or "we can't"
      - - Who would we work with on content and on the website?
        - Subject-matter experts, approvals and developer time
        - Named people and a release process, not "we'll figure it out"
      - - Is a redesign, replatform or URL change planned?
        - Migration risk, and whether to lead with migration protection
        - A launch date, and whether it's already fixed
      - - What would make this a success in a year, and who decides?
        - The target, and who really judges the program
        - A pipeline or lead target, and the name of the budget holder
      - - How do you buy services like this?
        - Budget, decision process and timing
        - A budget range, other decision-makers, and any procurement steps
  - type: table
    heading: Qualification
    intro: A program has to be able to show results, or it won't renew. Check these before proposing one.
    columns:
      - Criterion
      - How to check
      - If it's missing
    rows:
      - - "**Search demand in the category**"
        - Keyword volumes for category, problem and competitor terms in Ahrefs, Semrush or similar
        - Don't propose a search program. If buyers still research the category through AI assistants and review sites, the audit may still be worth selling.
      - - "**Deal value that can carry the fee**"
        - Typical deal size, and how many extra deals a year would cover the fee
        - Propose a smaller scope, or pass
      - - "**Access to analytics and the CMS**"
        - Who owns GA4, Search Console, the tag manager and the CMS, and whether we can have access in week one
        - Write into the scope that the audit presentation moves back by any days access is still missing after the first week
      - - "**Developer time**"
        - How technical changes get released, and how often
        - Sell the audit and the technical fixes as a defined project before a program
      - - "**A CRM that records lead source**"
        - How they'd know today that a lead came from search
        - Put the CRM source fields in the first 90 days, and say pipeline reporting starts once they're in place
      - - "**The budget holder involved**"
        - Who approves the budget, and whether they'll attend the audit presentation
        - Get them into the presentation, or expect the proposal to stall
  - type: ledger
    heading: Who we take on
    intro: Walking away from a poor fit protects the margin, the team's time and the agency's reputation. Most of these show up in the first call.
    left:
      title: Walk away, or sell only the audit
      items:
        - Wants guaranteed rankings or traffic numbers
        - Wants content volume with no subject-matter input
        - Asks for tactics that break platform rules, such as link schemes, fake reviews or review gating
        - Judges the program on traffic alone and won't discuss pipeline
        - Wants a program proposal before any diagnosis, to compare agencies on price
        - Has been through several agencies and can't say what went wrong
    right:
      title: Good fit
      items:
        - Sells a considered B2B purchase that buyers research before they talk to sales
        - Has a marketing leader who owns a pipeline target
        - Names competitors and shares CRM data
        - Has experts who will give time to content
        - Can get website changes released
        - Accepts the attribution ceiling once it's explained
  - type: table
    heading: Which offer to lead with
    intro: Lead with one offer. A menu of options invites the prospect to pick the cheapest.
    columns:
      - Situation
      - Lead with
      - Why
    rows:
      - - A small business needs a professional site live fast, with nobody to run it
        - "[KrateCMS Web Presence Starter](offer:kratecms)"
        - A set price, live in three business days, and managed for them
      - - They need a full marketing site their team can edit, without a custom build
        - "[WordPress site](offer:wordpress-site)"
        - The standard stack, theme and plugins make the price fixed and the launch predictable
      - - They have a website team but need senior engineering, design-system or web leadership help
        - "[Consulting](offer:consulting), scoped to the gap"
        - Scope the work their team can't do alone, and price it on scope, not hours
      - - Search matters, and there's no urgent event
        - "[Consulting](offer:consulting), starting with the audit"
        - Fixed scope and low risk, and its findings become the program proposal
      - - A redesign, replatform or URL change is planned
        - "[Consulting](offer:consulting), starting with migration protection"
        - The launch date sets the timeline, and a migration without a redirect map puts rankings at risk. Sell the audit after launch.
      - - Traffic or leads just dropped
        - The audit, starting with the matching [diagnostic](page:diagnostics)
        - They need an answer first. The diagnostic gives one early, and the audit shows what else is at risk.
      - - An executive is worried about what AI assistants say
        - The audit, leading with the AI visibility pillar
        - The prompt panel answers their question, and the other pillars show what's behind it
      - - They already have a recent audit they trust
        - "The 90-day program in [consulting](offer:consulting), starting by checking that audit against the six pillars"
        - Don't sell the same diagnosis twice. Confirm it still holds, and fill any gaps, such as AI visibility or measurement.
      - - They only want blog posts or a content calendar
        - The audit, or walk away
        - Content without a diagnosis is commodity SEO, and it won't show results the client will keep funding
  - type: panels
    heading: Pricing
    items:
      - eyebrow: Pricing
        title: Price on scope and revenue at stake, not hours
        text: The audit's opportunity model is the pricing argument for the program. A fee that's a small fraction of the expected-case upside is an easy decision; a fee that isn't needs a smaller scope, not a discount.
      - eyebrow: Pushback
        title: When the fee feels high
        text: Put the fee next to the expected case first. If it still doesn't fit, take plays out of the first 90 days, or start with the audit alone. Discounting the same scope tells the client the first number was padded.
  - type: table
    heading: Objections
    intro: The same objections come up on nearly every call. Most have a reason behind them; answer the reason, not the words.
    columns:
      - They say
      - What's usually behind it
      - How we answer
    rows:
      - - "\"SEO takes too long.\""
        - A past program showed no visible progress for months
        - "Show the play timings: measurement fixes show immediately, crawl fixes in days to weeks, page refreshes in weeks, new content in months. The roadmap puts quick wins first, and every play has its first-signal date written down before work starts."
      - - "\"Isn't SEO dead now that AI answers everything?\""
        - Fear that search is shrinking, or doubt that AI visibility is real
        - Both front doors depend on the same foundations, pages engines can crawl, understand and trust, and we report both side by side. Show them the prompt results for their category from your call preparation.
      - - "\"Our last agency didn't deliver.\""
        - They bought commodity SEO, with traffic reports and keyword lists but no pipeline
        - Ask what the last reports showed and what decisions they led to. Walk through the commodity and revenue-grade comparison on the [Overview](page:overview), and offer the audit as an independent second opinion.
      - - "\"We can do this in-house.\""
        - Cost, or a capable content team
        - Often true for writing and publishing. Sell the audit, then scope the program around what the team can't do alone, such as technical specifications, the prompt panel and the reporting.
      - - "\"Can you guarantee results?\""
        - They need to justify the spend internally
        - Nobody controls rankings or AI answers, and a guarantee is a warning sign. We commit to shipping the plays on schedule once the approvals, expert input and developer time they need arrive by the agreed dates, to writing the expected impact down in advance, and to reporting honestly against it. The opportunity model's range is their internal case.
      - - "\"It's too expensive.\""
        - The fee hasn't been compared with what's at stake
        - Put the fee next to the opportunity model's expected case. If it still doesn't make sense, reduce the scope, not the rate.
      - - "\"How will we know it's working?\""
        - Past reports showed traffic, never pipeline
        - Name the [attribution ceiling](principle:name-the-attribution-ceiling-on-day-one) now. Show the monthly report's chain from visibility to pipeline, and if the CRM doesn't record lead source, show that fixing it is in the first 90 days.
      - - "\"Let's wait until after the redesign.\""
        - They think search is a post-launch job
        - Before launch is the worst time to wait. A migration without a benchmark and a redirect map can lose rankings that take months to recover. Sell migration protection now, and the audit after launch.
      - - "\"Can't we just use AI to write more content?\""
        - Content volume is cheap now
        - Volume isn't what's missing. AI engines cite original data, named experts and clear facts, and summarize generic pages away. AI can speed up production; subject-matter input is what makes pages worth citing.
      - - "\"Just send us a proposal.\""
        - They're comparing agencies on price
        - A program proposal without a diagnosis is a guess. Send the audit's fixed scope and fee, which they can compare, and explain that the program proposal comes from its findings.
