heading: What every deliverable contains
lede: "Every phase ends in something the client keeps. Standard outlines make each deliverable the same quality on every account, quick to review, and easy for a new strategist to pick up. Most are living documents updated through the engagement, not new files each month. The monthly report's outline is in [Reporting](page:reporting)."
blocks:
  - type: strip
    label: Every deliverable follows the same rules
    items:
      - title: Answer first
        text: The first screen says what we found, what it means and what we recommend. Detail follows for readers who want it.
      - title: Evidence beside every claim
        text: Each number names its source, its date range and any caveat from the trust check.
      - title: An owner and a next step
        text: Every recommendation names who does it and what happens first, on the agency side and the client's.
      - title: One decision
        text: Ask for the decision you need, by a date. A deliverable without an ask gets read and filed.
      - title: Dated and versioned
        text: Every version carries a date and a version number. Earlier versions are kept, never overwritten.
  - type: table
    heading: The set
    intro: Fourteen deliverables cover all four offers. Living documents are started once and updated; the rest are issued at a point in time.
    columns:
      - Deliverable
      - When
      - Reader
      - Format
    rows:
      - - "**Audit report**"
        - "[Audit](phase:audit), issued once"
        - Marketing leader and executive sponsor
        - Document, presented live
      - - "**Competitor benchmark**"
        - Audit, refreshed each quarter
        - Marketing leader
        - Spreadsheet with a one-page summary
      - - "**Opportunity model**"
        - Audit, re-baselined each quarter
        - Marketing leader and finance
        - Spreadsheet
      - - "**Proposal**"
        - Audit, then each quarter as the renewal or expansion proposal
        - Budget holder
        - Document
      - - "**Scorecard**"
        - "[Baseline](phase:baseline), updated monthly"
        - Everyone on both sides
        - Spreadsheet or dashboard
      - - "**Measurement setup record**"
        - "Started at [onboarding](page:onboarding), completed in Baseline, updated when setup changes"
        - Marketing ops and the client's analytics owner
        - Document
      - - "**Keyword and prompt sets**"
        - Baseline, changed only as dated revisions
        - Marketing leader, who signs them off
        - Spreadsheet
      - - "**Roadmap**"
        - "[Plan](phase:plan), updated each sprint, re-planned each quarter"
        - Marketing leader and owners on both sides
        - Spreadsheet or project board
      - - "**Change log**"
        - "[Onboarding](page:onboarding) onward"
        - Account team and client
        - Shared spreadsheet
      - - "**Sprint notes**"
        - "[Execute](phase:execute), each sprint"
        - Owners on both sides
        - Short document
      - - "**Monthly report**"
        - "[Report](phase:report), monthly"
        - Marketing leader
        - Document with a 20-minute walkthrough
      - - "**Decisions log**"
        - "[Onboarding](page:onboarding) onward"
        - Marketing leader and account lead
        - Shared document
      - - "**Quarterly review deck**"
        - "[Review & renew](phase:review-renew), each quarter"
        - Leadership
        - Slides, presented live
      - - "**Migration pack**"
        - "[Migration protection](offer:migration-protection), tied to the launch date"
        - Marketing leader and the client's developers
        - Spreadsheets and a checklist
    after: "The phase checklists name some of these by stage. The baseline scorecard is the scorecard's first version. Target ranges and ownership are sections of the roadmap, and the updated and next-quarter roadmaps are later versions of it. The shipped-work log is the agency's part of the change log."
  - type: table
    heading: Audit report
    intro: The diagnosis, and usually the sale. Presented in a working session, then sent. Built from the [six-pillar audit](page:audit).
    columns:
      - Section
      - Contents
      - Question it answers
    rows:
      - - 1 · Summary
        - Where the site stands in three sentences, the upside range from the opportunity model, and the three findings that matter most
        - What's the verdict?
      - - 2 · Pillar scores
        - Each of the six pillars with its count of Pass, Watch and Fail, and a one-line verdict
        - Where are we strong, and where are we weak?
      - - 3 · Top ten findings
        - Each finding with its evidence, affected pages, revenue at stake as a range, confidence, effort and the play that fixes it, in ranked order
        - What's stopping us, and what is fixing it worth?
      - - 4 · Competitor position
        - "The benchmark summary: who wins which topics in search and in AI answers, and why"
        - Who is beating us?
      - - 5 · What we can't measure yet
        - Gaps from the measurement integrity pillar, and the cost of closing each
        - Which numbers shouldn't we trust yet?
      - - 6 · Recommended next step
        - The offer that addresses the findings, pointing to the proposal
        - What should we do now?
      - - Appendix
        - Every check with its score and evidence, and the findings below the top ten
        - Where's the detail?
  - type: table
    heading: Competitor benchmark
    intro: Three to five named competitors, agreed with the client, measured against the same keyword and prompt sets each quarter.
    columns:
      - Section
      - Contents
      - Question it answers
    rows:
      - - 1 · Who we compared
        - The named competitors, marked as search competitors, sales competitors or both
        - Who are we up against?
      - - 2 · Search share of voice
        - "[Share of voice](signal:share-of-voice) by topic and buyer stage, for the client and each competitor"
        - Who wins search, and where?
      - - 3 · AI mention and citation share
        - "[Mention and citation share](signal:citation-share) by engine and buyer stage"
        - Who wins AI answers?
      - - 4 · Cited sources
        - The most-cited domains in category answers, and which competitors each one mentions
        - Where do the engines get their answers?
      - - 5 · Authority
        - Relevant referring domains and review-site presence, new and lost since last quarter
        - Who is more credible beyond their own site?
      - - 6 · Why they win
        - "For the two or three topics worth contesting: better pages, more authority, or presence on the sources engines cite"
        - What would it take to win?
  - type: table
    heading: Opportunity model
    intro: The pricing argument for the program and the revenue input for ranking plays. Assumptions go on the first tab, before any number. The method is in [Opportunity model](signal:opportunity-value).
    columns:
      - Tab
      - Contents
      - Question it answers
    rows:
      - - Assumptions
        - "Lead value from the CRM (lead-to-opportunity rate, win rate and average deal size), click-through by position, and conversion rate by buyer stage, each with its source"
        - What are we assuming, and why?
      - - Topics
        - "For each priority topic: search demand, current and target position, and the expected clicks, leads and pipeline, in low, expected and high cases"
        - Where is the money?
      - - AI visibility
        - The leads AI referrals can plausibly drive on priority topics, in the same three cases
        - What is AI visibility worth?
      - - Summary
        - Totals as ranges, next to the program fee
        - Is the program worth funding?
      - - Versions
        - Each quarter's re-baseline, what changed in the assumptions, and past estimates against actual results
        - How good were our past estimates?
  - type: table
    heading: Proposal
    intro: Written from the audit's top ten findings, and reused each quarter as the renewal or expansion proposal. Keep it short and point to the audit for the detail.
    columns:
      - Section
      - Contents
      - Question it answers
    rows:
      - - 1 · What we found
        - The three findings that matter most, in the client's terms, with revenue at stake
        - Why act now?
      - - 2 · What we'll do
        - The plays in sequence, with the first 90 days spelled out
        - What will you actually do?
      - - 3 · What it's worth
        - The opportunity model's range next to the fee
        - Is it worth the money?
      - - 4 · What we need from you
        - Access, approvals, subject-matter experts, developer time and CRM source fields
        - What does it take from our side?
      - - 5 · How we'll report
        - The scorecard, the monthly report, and the attribution rule with its limits
        - How will we know it's working?
      - - 6 · Scope, timeline and fee
        - The offer, its length, what's out of scope, and how changes are priced
        - What exactly are we buying?
    after: For a renewal or expansion proposal, section 1 becomes results against the 90-day targets and the opportunity model, and section 2 becomes the next-quarter roadmap.
  - type: table
    heading: Scorecard
    intro: One row per metric across the seven layers of [the scorecard](page:overview). Recorded at baseline, updated every month, and shown as section 2 of the monthly report.
    columns:
      - Column
      - What to record
    rows:
      - - Layer and metric
        - The layer, and whether the metric is its north star, a leading indicator or a health check
      - - Baseline
        - The value, its date range and its source
      - - Target range
        - The 90-day range from the roadmap
      - - This period
        - The current value, and the change against baseline and against last year
      - - Status
        - On track, at risk or off track, with a one-line reason
      - - Caveat
        - Anything the trust check flagged for this metric this period
  - type: table
    heading: Measurement setup record
    intro: What was set up, where, when and by whom, so anyone new to the account can check it in minutes. It follows [Measurement](page:measurement).
    columns:
      - Section
      - Contents
    rows:
      - - Access
        - Who has access to Search Console, GA4, the tag manager, the CMS, the CDN or hosting, and CRM reports, at what level, and who granted it
      - - Setup items
        - Each item from the Measurement checklist with its status, the date it was done, who did it, and where to check it
      - - Definitions
        - The key events, the AI assistants channel rules, the content groups, and the current brand-term list version
      - - CRM
        - Which fields hold original source and landing page, and how many recent leads have them filled
      - - Attribution rule
        - The rule agreed in writing, the date, and who agreed it
      - - Known gaps
        - What can't be measured, since when, and the plan to close each gap
  - type: table
    heading: Keyword and prompt sets
    intro: The fixed denominators for share of voice and citation share. One spreadsheet with a tab for each set, signed off by the client. Changes are dated revisions, never silent edits.
    columns:
      - Column
      - What to record
    rows:
      - - Keyword or prompt
        - Written the way buyers search or ask
      - - Topic
        - The priority topic it belongs to
      - - Buyer stage
        - Problem, solution, comparison, brand or customer
      - - Target page
        - The page that should win it, or "gap" if none exists
      - - Version added
        - The version and date it joined the set
    after: A versions tab records each revision's date, what changed and why, and who signed it off.
  - type: table
    heading: Roadmap
    intro: Starts as the 90-day roadmap, is updated after each sprint, and is re-planned each quarter. One row per play.
    columns:
      - Column
      - What to record
    rows:
      - - Play
        - The play from the library, and the audit finding it addresses
      - - Priority
        - Revenue at stake, confidence, effort and the resulting rank. Blockers are marked and go first regardless of score.
      - - Expected impact
        - The signals it should move, as a range, and by when. Written before work starts.
      - - Owners
        - The agency owner and the client owner
      - - Client dependencies
        - Approvals, experts or developer time it needs, and the date they're needed
      - - Sprint and status
        - The planned sprint, and planned, in progress, shipped, or held for approval
      - - Result
        - Early signal against expected impact, filled in after it ships
    after: A targets tab holds the 90-day target range for each scorecard layer, and the scorecard reads its targets from there.
  - type: table
    heading: Change log
    intro: One shared log of everything that can move a number, whoever did it. It explains every movement in the monthly report and credits every win. Mirror entries as GA4 annotations.
    columns:
      - Column
      - What to record
    rows:
      - - Date
        - The day it went live, not the day it was planned
      - - Type
        - Content, technical, off-site, tracking, client release or Google update
      - - What changed
        - One line, with the pages or template affected
      - - Who
        - The agency, the client's team, or outside (Google, a vendor)
      - - Expected effect
        - For our work, the signal it should move and when the first signal should show
      - - Link
        - The ticket, the page or the announcement
      - - Early signal
        - What moved, filled in at the next monthly report
  - type: table
    heading: Sprint notes
    intro: One short note per sprint, sent when it starts and closed when it ends. Readable in two minutes.
    columns:
      - Section
      - Contents
    rows:
      - - This sprint
        - The plays in scope from the roadmap, and what counts as shipped for each
      - - Needed from the client
        - Approvals, experts and developer time, each with a date
      - - Shipped
        - What went live, with each item added to the change log
      - - Held
        - "What didn't ship and why: waiting on approval, blocked, or deprioritized"
      - - Early signals
        - Anything the leading indicators already show, good or bad
      - - Next sprint
        - What's planned, and what needs to be ready for it
  - type: table
    heading: Decisions log
    intro: Every decision the client makes, so nobody re-argues it three months later. The decision each monthly report asks for is recorded here.
    columns:
      - Column
      - What to record
    rows:
      - - Date
        - When it was decided
      - - Decision
        - What was decided, in one sentence
      - - Options considered
        - What else was on the table, and the tradeoff
      - - Decided by
        - The name and role of the person who decided
      - - Why
        - The reason given, and the evidence it rested on
      - - Follow-up
        - What changes in the roadmap, and when to revisit it
  - type: table
    heading: Quarterly review deck
    intro: Presented live to leadership. Argued in pipeline and competitive position, not tasks completed. Built from the scorecard, the benchmark, the opportunity model and the roadmap.
    columns:
      - Slide
      - Contents
      - Question it answers
    rows:
      - - 1 · The quarter
        - Results against the 90-day targets, and the one decision needed
        - Did it work?
      - - 2 · Pipeline
        - Search-sourced leads and pipeline against target and program cost
        - Is it paying off?
      - - 3 · Competitive position
        - Share of voice and citation share against named competitors, this quarter against last
        - Are we winning the market?
      - - 4 · What shipped and what it did
        - Plays shipped, expected impact against results, and what the misses taught us
        - Did the plan hold up?
      - - 5 · The opportunity, re-baselined
        - The revised opportunity model, and what changed in its assumptions
        - What's left to win?
      - - 6 · Next quarter
        - The revised roadmap, with target ranges and client dependencies
        - What happens next?
      - - 7 · Scope
        - Proposed scope changes, each with its revenue case
        - Where should we invest more?
  - type: table
    heading: Migration pack
    intro: The deliverables of [Migration protection](offer:migration-protection), in the order they're needed. Each goes to the client's developers as well as the marketing leader. The steps are in [Protect a migration](play:protect-migration).
    columns:
      - Item
      - Contents
      - When
    rows:
      - - Pre-launch benchmark
        - Rankings, clicks, indexed pages and leads for priority pages, and a full crawl of the current site
        - As soon as the engagement starts, and before launch
      - - Redirect map
        - Every old URL with traffic, links or rankings, mapped to its new URL, with priority pages flagged
        - Before the staging review
      - - Staging crawl report
        - Missing pages, broken redirects, changed titles and content, noindex or robots rules, and internal links to priority pages
        - Before launch sign-off
      - - Launch-day checklist
        - Redirects tested, robots.txt and noindex settings checked, sitemap submitted, tracking firing once, priority pages inspected
        - Launch day
      - - Monitoring log
        - Index coverage, clicks, errors and leads against the benchmark, with issues found and fixed
        - Daily for four weeks after launch
  - type: flow
    heading: Before anything goes to the client
    intro: A second person on the team reviews every deliverable before it's sent. Catching an error here is cheaper than correcting it after the client finds it.
    items:
      - title: Check the numbers
        text: Spot-check figures against their source for the same date range, and confirm the trust check ran for the period.
      - title: Check the evidence
        text: Every finding and claim points to its evidence. Cut anything that can't be backed up.
      - title: Check the ask
        text: Each recommendation has an owner and a next step, and the decision needed is stated once, with a date.
      - title: Read it as the client
        text: Replace agency shorthand with plain words, spell product and competitor names correctly, and remove internal notes.
      - title: Version and file it
        text: Date and version it, save it in the client's folder, and add any decisions to the decisions log.
