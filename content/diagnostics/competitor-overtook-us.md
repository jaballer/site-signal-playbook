title: A competitor overtook us
inTheField: Share of voice on the priority topic set has moved four points to one competitor over a quarter, with no drop in the client's own rankings. They didn't take our positions. They took positions we never held, on questions we never answered, and the client's leadership found out from a sales rep rather than from us.
steps:
  - title: Check it against the fixed keyword and prompt sets
    detail: Share of voice only means something against a frozen set. If the set changed this quarter, the movement may be an artifact of what you chose to count, and that has to be ruled out first.
  - title: Separate positions lost from ground never held
    detail: Queries where the client fell and the competitor rose is a defensive problem on existing pages. Queries where the competitor rose and the client never ranked is a coverage gap. The two need different plays and different budgets.
  - title: Find what they shipped
    detail: Crawl their site against a crawl from three months ago, or use a third-party tool's new-pages report. Look for new page types as much as new pages, such as a comparison set, a template for integrations, a data study or a glossary.
  - title: Check the off-site move
    detail: New referring domains, a review-volume surge, a new category badge, an analyst mention, or inclusion in the lists that engines cite. In established categories this moves faster than anything on their site.
  - title: Check whether it's visible in AI answers too
    detail: Run the prompt set. A competitor gaining in both search and AI answers is executing. A competitor gaining only in AI answers has usually won a source rather than a page.
  - title: Decide whether to contest or to route around
    detail: Contesting a page a competitor owns with a genuinely better asset is expensive and slow. Often the better return is the adjacent topic they've left open. Rank both options in the opportunity model and let the number decide, rather than the client's pride.
