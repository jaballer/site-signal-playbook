---
order: 7
question: What should we write, update or retire?
askedBy: The content lead planning next quarter, or a CMO asking why the blog doesn't sell.
timebox: Half a day
expect: A decision for every priority page and gap (create, refresh, consolidate or retire), ranked by value.
steps:
  - "Start from the topic coverage map: every priority topic by buyer stage, with its target page or a gap."
  - In Search Console, compare each page's clicks with the same period last year to find decaying pages.
  - "Find cannibalization: queries where two or more of the client's pages rank. In Search Console, click a query, then open the **Pages** tab."
  - In GA4's **Landing page** report, add key events to see which content produces leads, not just visits.
  - Give each page a decision, and size it with the opportunity model.
read:
  - Gaps at the comparison, alternatives and pricing stages are usually worth more than another top-of-funnel post.
  - Refreshing a decaying page that still matches intent usually pays off faster than publishing a new one.
  - Retiring or merging thin pages is a real recommendation. Fewer, stronger pages are easier for search engines and AI to trust.
beforeYouStart: Agree priority topics and buyer stages with the client first. Without them, content decisions turn into a list of keyword volumes.
script: We recommend creating [N] pages for [topics], refreshing [N] decaying pages led by [page], and consolidating [N] overlapping pages into [N]. Together they address [X]% of the opportunity in [topic].
signals:
  - topic-coverage
  - content-decay
  - cannibalization
  - content-quality
  - extractability
plays:
  - refresh-decaying-pages
  - consolidate-cannibalization
  - topic-cluster
  - definition-pages
  - author-expertise
