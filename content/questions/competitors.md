---
order: 4
question: Who is beating us, and why?
askedBy: The marketing lead or CEO after losing deals to a competitor who 'shows up everywhere'.
timebox: 2 hours
expect: "Which competitors win search and AI visibility on each priority topic, and the specific reasons: better pages, more authority, or presence on the sources engines trust."
steps:
  - In the rank tracker, compare share of voice for three to five named competitors across the keyword set, by topic and buyer stage.
  - "For the topics you're losing, open the pages that outrank the client. Note what they have that the client's page doesn't: a comparison table, original data, depth, freshness, stronger internal links."
  - In a backlink tool, compare relevant referring domains, and look at the links the winning pages earned.
  - From the AI prompt runs, list which competitors are named in category answers, and which sources cite them.
read:
  - Competitors rarely win everywhere. Find the two or three topics where they win and why, then decide which are worth contesting.
  - If competitors win with pages the client doesn't have, it's a content gap. If they win with similar pages, it's authority or sources.
  - The competitor that dominates AI answers is often not the one that dominates Google. Report the two separately.
beforeYouStart: Agree the named competitors with the client. Search competitors, the sites ranking for your terms, often differ from sales competitors, and both matter.
script: "[Competitor] leads on [topic] with [X]% share of voice against our [X]%, mainly because [reason]. In AI answers, [competitor] is named in [X]% of category prompts, largely through [sources]. We'll contest [topic] first; [topic] isn't worth the cost yet."
signals:
  - share-of-voice
  - topic-coverage
  - referring-domains
  - cited-sources
diagnostics:
  - competitor-overtook-us
plays:
  - comparison-pages
  - topic-cluster
  - win-cited-sources
---
