SEO and AI search visibility

Search has two front doors now: the results page and the AI answer.

This is the operating playbook for an SEO and AI search agency working on B2B marketing websites. It covers how we diagnose where a site stands, decide what's worth doing, ship it in the right order, and report the result in terms a CMO and a CFO both accept. It's written as repeatable procedure, so every client gets the same rigor no matter who runs the account.

Who it's for

The account team, and the client's marketing leader

Strategists and analysts use it to run every engagement the same way. Marketing leaders use it to see what they're buying, what they'll get, and when.

What it answers

The four questions every SEO buyer asks

Are we visible where buyers look? Are we beating competitors there? Is it producing pipeline? What should we do next, and what will it return?

What's in it

Nine chapters

Leader questions with step-by-step answers, the engagement model and offers, a six-pillar audit, a play library, four diagnostic flows, the reporting model, a 38-signal catalog, measurement foundations, and the principles behind all of it.

The value chain

Every engagement works the same chain, in order. When a number moves, it's diagnosed in the link where it moved, not blamed on "SEO".

1FoundationsCan Google and AI crawlers reach, render and understand the site?
2AuthorityDoes the site have the best page for each topic, and credibility beyond its own domain?
3VisibilityDo we appear for buyers' questions, in results pages and AI answers, against competitors?
4TrafficDoes that visibility turn into qualified visits, including from AI assistants?
5PipelineDo those visits become leads and opportunities sales accepts?
Integrity sits under all five: can every number in the report be trusted this month?
“

Clicks are no longer a complete measure of search performance. When an AI answer resolves a question on the results page, the brand it cites still wins the buyer's attention, and the brand it leaves out loses it without a trace in analytics. A program that measures only clicks will under-report its wins and miss its losses.

Commodity SEO or a revenue-grade program

Clients have usually bought the first kind before. The second is what this playbook exists to deliver, and the difference is visible in the first month.

Commodity SEO

  • Reports traffic and rankings, and goes quiet when AI answers cut clicks
  • Tracks keywords nobody mapped to a page, a buyer stage or a competitor
  • Publishes on a content calendar instead of against gaps competitors are winning
  • Treats AI visibility as a buzzword, or ignores it
  • Can't say which pages produce pipeline
  • Ships fixes in bulk, so nothing can be credited
  • Reports numbers without checking the tracking first

Revenue-grade program

  • Reports the whole chain from visibility to pipeline, with AI answers next to results pages
  • Maps every priority topic to a page, a buyer stage and a competitor benchmark
  • Puts content where demand exists and competitors are beatable
  • Measures citation share on a fixed prompt set, monthly, against competitors
  • Ties landing pages to leads and opportunities
  • Ships in sequenced sprints, with the expected impact written down first
  • Starts every report with an integrity check

Audit framework

Six pillars, scored with evidence

The audit answers one question: what's stopping this site from being the answer buyers find, in search results and in AI assistants, and what is fixing it worth? Every check is scored Pass, Watch or Fail with evidence. The fails then become a short, ranked list of findings with revenue attached.

ScoreMeansWhat to write
PassMeets the standard on every priority page or templateOne line of evidence. Don't pad the report with passes.
WatchPartly meets it, or is fine now but at riskWhat would turn it into a fail, and when to re-check
FailBlocks visibility, traffic or trustworthy measurement on priority pagesEvidence, affected pages, revenue at stake, and the play that fixes it

From checks to findings

Nobody buys a checklist. The audit is presented as the ten findings that matter, in order.

Group fails into findings

Several failed checks usually share one cause: a template, a rendering approach, a missing page type. One cause, one finding.

Attach revenue at stake

Use the opportunity model: the leads the affected topics or pages could produce, as a range.

Rate confidence and effort

How sure is the diagnosis, and what does the fix take across agency and client?

Rank and cut to ten

Blockers first, then by revenue at stake × confidence ÷ effort. Everything else goes in an appendix.

Name the play and the owner

Each finding maps to a play, an owner and a first step. That mapping is the bridge from audit to proposal.

Play library

The standard plays, and how to choose between them

Every roadmap is built from the same plays. Each lists when to run it, what we do, which signals it should move, and how long before the first signal shows. Candidates are ranked with the model below, so the roadmap is argued in revenue rather than best practice.

×

Priority = revenue at stake × confidence ÷ effort. Blockers to crawling, indexing or honest measurement go first regardless of score, because they cap the return on everything else.

Revenue at stake

From the opportunity model

Leads the play could add or protect over 12 months, multiplied by lead value. Use the expected case, and show the range.

Confidence

How sure is the diagnosis?

High (0.9): direct evidence and a precedent. Medium (0.6): a reasonable inference. Low (0.3): a bet worth testing small.

Effort

Both sides of the table

S: under a week of combined effort. M: one to four weeks. L: more than a month, or ongoing. Count client approvals and developer time.

Plays

Diagnostic flows

When a number moves the wrong way

Four situations that recur on every account. Each is a fixed order of checks, because the cheapest checks most often end the investigation, and because the expensive ones produce confident wrong answers when run first.

What this looks like in the field: leadership sees organic sessions down year over year and asks whether SEO stopped working. Split apart, it's several smaller stories. Branded demand softened with the market. A group of informational queries gained AI Overviews and lost clicks at stable rankings. One template lost indexing after a release. And part of the "drop" is a tracking change in GA4.
Confirm it's real, and where it's measured

Compare Search Console clicks with GA4 organic sessions for the same period. If Search Console is flat and GA4 fell, the cause is tracking or consent, not search.

Split branded from non-branded

A branded decline is a brand and demand question. Only a non-branded decline belongs to the search program.

Find the pages and queries that lost clicks

In Search Console, compare periods on the Pages and Queries tabs and sort by clicks lost. Declines are almost always concentrated.

Separate ranking loss from click loss

For the losing queries, did position fall, or did CTR fall at the same position? Stable position with falling CTR points to AI Overviews or new results-page features. Look at the results page.

Check the timeline

Line the drop up against Google's Search Status Dashboard, site releases, migrations, and robots or CDN changes.

Check the losing pages for technical regressions

Indexing status, canonicals, noindex tags, rendering and internal links on the pages that fell.

Report the components, then the plan

Present the drop as its parts, each with an owner and a response, rather than one number and one explanation.

What this looks like in the field: a stakeholder forwards a screenshot of an assistant recommending three competitors and not the client. The prompt panel shows citation share on category prompts slipping over two months, concentrated in one engine. In the same window, a competitor published a comparison page, and a widely cited review site refreshed its category list without the client.
Check it against the prompt set, not the screenshot

AI answers vary from run to run, so one answer proves nothing. Confirm the change across the full set and at least two runs.

Split by engine and by prompt type

A drop in one engine points to that engine's sources. A drop across engines on comparison prompts points to the category's sources changing.

Compare the cited sources before and after

New domains being cited is the most common cause. The list tells you where to act.

Check access and accuracy

Confirm AI crawlers still reach priority pages (robots.txt, CDN rules, logs), and that nothing important moved behind JavaScript, a login or a redirect.

Check what competitors shipped

New comparison pages, studies, review campaigns or list placements in the window.

Respond at the source

Win back the cited sources, publish the page the answers are missing, and correct stale facts. Re-run the affected prompts monthly.

What this looks like in the field: leadership arrives with one total: organic leads down while traffic is flat. Split by form and landing page, a newly added popup is growing while two embedded forms fall away. Meanwhile, conversion-event volume spiked on exactly the days the tag container was published. Neither fact is visible in the total, and both change what to fix.
Validate the baseline before the decline

Overlay daily conversion volume on the change log. Spikes within a few days of a tag publish or release are testing or double-firing, not demand, and they inflate the baseline.

Check for duplicate firing

Run Tag Assistant on conversion and thank-you pages. Tags firing twice mean every conversion number is wrong.

Break the total down by form and landing page

A uniform decline points to something sitewide: tracking, consent, speed. A concentrated one points to specific pages.

Check the traffic mix

Flat sessions can hide a shift: more informational traffic from new content converts at a lower rate than product-page traffic. Compare conversion rate by buyer stage.

Test whether leads just moved

If the number of people submitting any form is flat, a new form or popup is taking submissions from the old ones.

Only then check speed, form errors and CRM sync

All real causes, and all rarely the first one.

What this looks like in the field: a release changes a template's canonical tags, or a staging noindex setting ships to production. Over a few weeks a whole section moves to excluded in Search Console, and traffic erodes slowly enough that nobody connects it to the release.
Find the reason in Search Console

Indexing → Pages, looking at which exclusion reasons grew. The reason names the investigation.

Inspect a sample of affected URLs

URL Inspection: live test against the indexed version, declared versus Google-selected canonical, crawl allowed, page fetch.

Match it to a template and a release

Exclusions clustered on one template almost always trace to a release that changed it.

Check the usual causes

Noindex tags, canonicals pointing elsewhere, robots.txt rules, redirects, soft 404s, rendering failures. If the main reason is "Crawled – currently not indexed" or a duplicate-canonical reason, the fix is usually consolidation or better content, not a technical switch.

Fix at the template, then validate

Use Validate fix in the Indexing report, and request indexing on priority URLs.

Add a release check

Put a pre-launch crawl of changed templates into the client's release process so it doesn't happen again.

Reporting when clicks fall and visibility rises

AI Overviews and assistants answer questions before a click. Report impressions, citation share and branded demand as leading indicators next to clicks, and set that expectation at kickoff, not in the month traffic dips. Measure the effect directly: group priority keywords by whether an AI Overview appears, and compare CTR at similar positions.

The attribution ceiling

Last-click analytics undercounts search's role in long B2B buying journeys, and AI answers that influence a buyer without a click don't appear at all. Say this in the first review, not the fourth. Two honest options:

Option A

GA4 plus CRM original source

Leads by landing page from GA4, pipeline by original source from the CRM. Directional, cheap, and enough for most programs.

Option B

Warehouse join

GA4's BigQuery export joined to CRM records for journey-level attribution. Solid, and the only way to show multi-touch influence. Costs setup and analyst time.

Agree the attribution rule in writing before month one. Changing it mid-program looks like moving the goalposts, even when it's the right call.

If the client has no tracking plan

Two events cover most of what a search program needs to report. Define them once and never invent new event names inside a template.

EventFires whenParameters
form_submitA lead form succeeds (the provider's success callback or the thank-you page)form_variant, form_location, source_cta
cta_clickA primary call to action is clickedcta_text, cta_position, page_location

Register the parameters as custom dimensions the day the events ship; custom dimensions don't backfill. Never put UTM parameters on links between pages of the client's own site: they overwrite the real traffic source and corrupt channel reporting.

Principles

What holds across every engagement

Core Web Vitals thresholds follow Google's published guidance (LCP ≤ 2.5s, INP ≤ 200ms, CLS ≤ 0.1 at the 75th percentile). Tool names are examples, not endorsements. Field notes are generalized patterns from B2B engagements, not any single client's figures. Menu paths in Google tools change; verify them when something looks different.
